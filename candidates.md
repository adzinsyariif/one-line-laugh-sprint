# Candidate lines — One-Line Laugh Sprint

Everyday life, light tone, one line each, twist by the last word.

1. I put my phone on airplane mode to save battery, and now it has more travel plans than I do.
2. My houseplant is the only thing in my life I've kept alive this long, and it's plastic.
3. I bought a gym membership in January; by February it was just a monthly donation to a building full of mirrors.
4. My to-do list is just a diary of things I confidently believed I would do yesterday.
5. My gym charges me monthly for the guilt; the workouts, apparently, are sold separately.
6. I meal-prep every Sunday like a responsible grown-up, and by Wednesday night I am eating cereal over the kitchen sink like a raccoon.
7. I bought a planner to get my life together; it is now the most organised thing I own and the least opened.
8. Adulthood is mostly saying "we really should do this again" to a pile of laundry.

## Pick

Line 6's image is the freshest — the raccoon over the sink — but it stops one beat
short: the raccoon needs a stake in adult life to make the contrast land. Adding
"with a mortgage" gives the last-word payoff the judge's brief asks for, and
tightening the front half ("responsible adult", semicolon, drop "night"/"kitchen")
keeps it neat read aloud. Chosen line develops 6 toward that.
